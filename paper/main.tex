\title{PCA-Driven Adaptive Sensor Triage for Edge AI Inference}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Ankit Hemant Lade}
\IEEEauthorblockA{}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Multi-channel sensor networks in industrial IoT generate data volumes that often exceed available bandwidth. We propose \textbf{PCA-Triage}, a lightweight streaming algorithm that converts incremental PCA loadings into proportional per-channel sampling rates under a total bandwidth budget. At each time window, PCA-Triage (1) updates an incremental PCA model, (2) computes channel importance via weighted loadings $s_j = \sum_i \sigma_i |V_{ij}|^2$, and (3) allocates sampling rates proportional to importance subject to a budget constraint and minimum rate floor. The algorithm runs at $O(wdk)$ per window with zero trainable parameters, requiring 0.67\,ms per decision on a single CPU core. We evaluate PCA-Triage on 7 real-world benchmark datasets spanning chemical processes (TEP, 52 sensors), server monitoring (SMD, 38 sensors; PSM, 25 sensors), spacecraft telemetry (MSL, 55 sensors), industrial control (HAI, 82 sensors), water treatment (SKAB, 8 sensors), and bearing degradation (NASA, 16 sensors), against 5 baselines. PCA-Triage is the best unsupervised method on 3 of 6 real-world datasets at 50\% bandwidth, and within 2\% of the best on 5 of 6, achieving F1\,=\,0.961 on TEP (matching full-data performance) while maintaining fault detection accuracy above 0.90 across bandwidth levels as low as 30\%. The algorithm adapts to fault onset within 1--3 windows and scales to 500 channels under the 5\,ms edge deployment target.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Consider a chemical processing plant instrumented with 200 sensors monitoring temperatures, pressures, flow rates, and valve positions. Each sensor streams data at 1\,Hz, generating 1.2\,MB per minute. An edge gateway must relay this data over a constrained industrial network to a centralized fault detection system. The available bandwidth supports only 50\% of the full data volume. The operator faces a deceptively simple question: \textit{which sensors should receive more of the limited bandwidth, and which can tolerate lower sampling rates without compromising fault detection?}

The naive answer---sample every sensor at a uniformly reduced rate---wastes bandwidth on channels that carry redundant or uninformative signals. The ideal allocation is \textit{adaptive}: it shifts bandwidth toward informative channels as operating conditions change.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

We formalize this as the \textit{sensor triage} problem. Given $d$ sensor channels and a total bandwidth budget $B \in (0, 1]$, allocate a per-channel sampling rate $r_j \in [r_{\min}, 1]$ for each channel $j = 1, \ldots, d$, subject to:
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{d} \sum_{j=1}^{d} r_j \leq B
\end{equation}
such that a downstream fault detection model trained on triaged data maintains accuracy close to one trained on full-rate data. The allocation must be updated online as new data arrives.

This problem sits at the intersection of three well-studied areas---yet none addresses it directly. \textit{PCA-based process monitoring}~\cite{chen2013pca, processmonitoring2024survey, downs1993tep} uses principal component loadings for post-hoc fault diagnosis only. \textit{Streaming PCA algorithms}~\cite{weng2003ccipca, balzano2010grouse, yang2018historypca} output basis matrices rather than channel-level decisions. \textit{Adaptive sampling in IoT}~\cite{benaboud2021adaptive, giordano2023energy} applies the same rate to every channel.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Algorithm.} We introduce PCA-Triage, the first streaming algorithm that converts incremental PCA loadings into proportional per-channel sampling rates under a bandwidth budget. The algorithm is unsupervised and runs at $O(wdk)$ per window.

\item \textbf{Empirical validation.} We evaluate on 7 real-world datasets (8--82 sensors) against 5 baselines, showing PCA-Triage is the best unsupervised method on 3 of 6 datasets at 50\% bandwidth.

\item \textbf{Edge viability.} PCA-Triage completes each decision in 0.67\,ms on a single CPU core, adapts to fault onset within 1--3 windows, and scales to 500 channels under the 5\,ms edge target.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

We organize the literature along two axes---\textit{static vs.\ streaming} and \textit{fixed-rule vs.\ data-driven}---to identify the gap our method fills.

\subsection{PCA for Process Monitoring}

PCA has been the workhorse of industrial process monitoring for over two decades~\cite{chen2013pca, processmonitoring2024survey}. The standard approach monitors Hotelling's $T^2$ and Squared Prediction Error (SPE) statistics. On the Tennessee Eastman Process~\cite{downs1993tep, rieth2017tep}, PCA-based methods achieve fault detection rates exceeding 93\%~\cite{chen2013pca}. Loading vectors reveal which sensors contribute most to each principal component---used for \textit{post-hoc} fault diagnosis~\cite{mnassri2015feature}.

However, all existing PCA-based methods assume full-resolution data is available. None address bandwidth-constrained scenarios where the system must decide \textit{which channels to prioritize before data collection}.

\subsection{Streaming PCA and Online Feature Selection}

Oja's rule~\cite{oja1982simplified} provides the foundational online PCA update. CCIPCA~\cite{weng2003ccipca} extends to multiple components at $O(dk)$ cost. GROUSE~\cite{balzano2010grouse} tracks subspaces on the Grassmannian manifold. History PCA~\cite{yang2018historypca} achieves faster convergence. Balzano et al.~\cite{balzano2018streaming} survey these algorithms comprehensively. Recent work on low-precision streaming PCA~\cite{lowprecision2025streaming} demonstrates continued algorithmic progress.

Online feature selection methods---SAOLA~\cite{yu2014saola}, OSFS~\cite{wu2013osfs}, OSFSW~\cite{you2018osfsw}---produce \textit{binary} include/exclude decisions and require supervised labels~\cite{zaman2022streaming}. Even the most recent OSSFS~\cite{zhou2025ossfs} (2025) remains binary and supervised.

None of these methods produce \textit{proportional} per-channel sampling rates under a budget constraint.

\subsection{Adaptive Sampling in IoT}

Ben-Aboud et al.~\cite{benaboud2021adaptive} use Kalman predictions to adapt temporal sampling intervals. Giordano et al.~\cite{giordano2023energy} achieve 85--95\% of optimal throughput with 527 CPU cycles per day. ML-DSRA~\cite{mldsra2024parametric} auto-tunes temporal sampling parameters. Compressive sensing~\cite{cs2017survey} exploits signal sparsity but requires expensive reconstruction.

All methods treat sensors identically---the same temporal rate for every channel. Our method answers \textit{which channels} deserve more bandwidth.

\subsection{Edge AI}

Edge AI research~\cite{gill2024edgeai} focuses on model-side optimization (compression, distillation, split computing). Attention mechanisms~\cite{cao2021mafs, vtt2024transformer, xu2023dcff} learn channel importance but at $O(d^2)$ cost with hundreds of thousands of parameters---too expensive for edge deployment.

Our method optimizes the \textit{data side}---complementary to model compression. PCA-Triage runs at $O(wdk)$ with zero trainable parameters.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Channel Importance Scoring}

For a window $\mathbf{X}_w \in \mathbb{R}^{w \times d}$, PCA-Triage fits IncrementalPCA to extract loadings $\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times d}$ and singular values $\boldsymbol{\sigma} \in \mathbb{R}^k$. The importance score for channel $j$:
\begin{equation}
s_j = \sum_{i=1}^{k} \sigma_i \cdot V_{ij}^2
\label{eq:importance}
\end{equation}

Scores are smoothed via exponential moving average with forgetting factor $\lambda$:
\begin{equation}
\bar{s}_j^{(t)} = \lambda \cdot \bar{s}_j^{(t-1)} + (1 - \lambda) \cdot s_j^{(t)}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Rate Allocation}

Given smoothed scores $\bar{\mathbf{s}}$ and budget $B$:
\begin{equation}
r_j = r_{\min} + \frac{\bar{s}_j}{\sum_{j'} \bar{s}_{j'}} \cdot (B - r_{\min}) \cdot d
\end{equation}
with clipping to $[r_{\min}, 1]$.

\subsection{Algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{PCA-Triage}
\label{alg:pcatriage}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Stream of windows $\mathbf{X}_w$, budget $B$, components $k$, forgetting $\lambda$
\STATE Initialize IncrementalPCA($k$), $\bar{\mathbf{s}} \gets$ None
\FOR{each window $\mathbf{X}_w$}
  \STATE \texttt{partial\_fit}($\mathbf{X}_w$) $\to \mathbf{V}, \boldsymbol{\sigma}$
  \STATE $s_j \gets \sum_i \sigma_i V_{ij}^2$ \hfill $\triangleright$ Importance
  \STATE $\bar{\mathbf{s}} \gets \lambda \bar{\mathbf{s}} + (1-\lambda) \cdot \text{normalize}(\mathbf{s})$ \hfill $\triangleright$ Smooth
  \STATE $r_j \gets r_{\min} + (\bar{s}_j / \sum \bar{s}) \cdot (B - r_{\min}) \cdot d$ \hfill $\triangleright$ Allocate
  \STATE Clip $r_j$ to $[r_{\min}, 1]$
  \STATE Keep sample $x_t[j]$ with probability $r_j$ \hfill $\triangleright$ Acquire
  \STATE Forward-fill NaN values \hfill $\triangleright$ Reconstruct
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Complexity}

Time: $O(wdk)$ per window. Memory: $O(wd + kd)$. Parameters: 0 (computed, not trained). For TEP ($d{=}52$, $k{=}10$, $w{=}50$): 26,000 FLOPs per window.

\subsection{Experiment 3: Adaptivity Under Fault Onset}

When TEP Fault~1 (A/C feed ratio disturbance) occurs, PCA-Triage shifts bandwidth toward the responsible channels within 1--3 windows ($\lambda{=}0.85$). The A~Feed Flow valve (xmv\_3) jumps from 9\% to 38\% sampling rate after onset.

\subsection{Experiment 4: Ablation Studies}

Ablation over hyperparameters shows robustness: F1 varies $<$2\% for $k \in [3, 10]$, $w \in [50, 500]$. The forgetting factor $\lambda$ trades accuracy ($\lambda{=}1.0$: F1\,=\,0.962) for reaction speed ($\lambda{=}0.85$: 0--3 window reaction).

\subsection{Experiment 5: Computational Cost}

PCA-Triage completes each decision in \textbf{0.67\,ms} (single-threaded), well under the 5\,ms edge target. It scales to 500 channels at $<$4\,ms. Memory footprint: 8.5\,MB with 520 computed parameters---orders of magnitude lighter than attention-based alternatives (325K+ parameters, 51\,ms per decision).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{When PCA-Triage excels.} On datasets with high inter-channel correlation (TEP, SMD, PSM), PCA captures redundancy that variance-based methods miss. The advantage is most pronounced at low bandwidth (10--30\%).

\textbf{When PCA-Triage struggles.} On SKAB (8 sensors, limited correlation) and HAI (near-perfect detection for all methods), the margin over simpler baselines narrows. On datasets where labels are available, supervised Mutual Information outperforms.

\textbf{The $\lambda$ trade-off.} $\lambda{=}1.0$ gives best F1 but slow fault reaction (19 windows). $\lambda{=}0.85$ reacts in 0--3 windows but sacrifices ${\sim}2\%$ F1. This is a deployment-specific choice.

\textbf{Limitations.} (1)~The algorithm assumes inter-channel correlations exist; on independent channels, it reduces to variance-based allocation. (2)~The minimum rate $r_{\min}$ consumes fixed bandwidth overhead. (3)~Forward-fill reconstruction introduces lag at very low budgets ($<$20\%).

\textbf{Future work.} Federated PCA across distributed edge nodes; adaptive $k$ selection via cumulative variance thresholding; integration with temporal adaptive sampling (DSRA, SoD) for joint spatial-temporal optimization; contribution of a \texttt{StreamingPCASelector} transformer to the Feature-engine library.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented PCA-Triage, the first streaming algorithm that converts incremental PCA loadings into proportional per-channel bandwidth allocation for sensor networks. On 7 real-world benchmarks spanning chemical processes, server monitoring, spacecraft telemetry, and industrial control, PCA-Triage is the best unsupervised triage method on 3 of 6 datasets at 50\% bandwidth, achieving F1\,=\,0.961 on TEP while running in 0.67\,ms per decision. The algorithm adapts to fault onset within 1--3 windows and scales to 500 channels under the 5\,ms edge deployment target. PCA-Triage bridges the gap between PCA-based monitoring and adaptive data acquisition---enabling bandwidth-efficient sensor networks without sacrificing fault detection accuracy.
